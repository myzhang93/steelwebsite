# 201 Stainless Steel: The Smart Economical Austenitic Workhorse

## 1. 201 in a Nutshell
201 stainless steel is a manganese-bearing austenitic alloy developed in the 1950s as a nickel-saving alternative to 304. By substituting part of the nickel with 4–6 % manganese, the grade keeps an austenitic structure yet lowers raw-material cost ≈ 25-30 %. The result is a corrosion-resistant steel that is non-magnetic in the annealed condition, can be deep-drawn, brake-formed, roll-formed and welded with the same ease as 304, while offering 15-20 % higher yield strength—an advantage when weight or section reduction is important.

The higher work-hardening exponent (n ≈ 0.35) lets 201 reach 1000 MPa after 40 % cold reduction—ideal for roll-formed roofing, crash-resistant automotive rails or high-tensile banding.

## 4. Corrosion Performance – Where It Excels & Where It Stops
**Atmospheric & mild media**  
- Rural, urban, light-industrial: on par with 304 for the first 5-10 years; slight tea-staining later if unwashed.  
- Food acids (acetic, citric, lactic): acceptable up to 60 °C.  
- Oxidising acids (nitric 20 % 60 °C): good; used in fertilizer mixer drums.

**Limitations**  
- Chloride pitting resistance PRE ≈ 17 vs 304 ≈ 19; avoid permanent immersion in &gt;200 ppm Cl⁻ &gt;40 °C.  
- Coastal splash zones require periodic wash-off or upgrade to 316.  
- Stress-corrosion cracking threshold in MgCl₂ ≈ 120 MPa—lower than 304 (150 MPa) but adequate for most indoor fabrications.

**Guideline**  
Indoors or rinsed outdoors: 201 = 304.  
Continuous marine wetting: choose 316L or 2205.

## 5. Weldability & Fabrication Tips
- All arc processes: TIG, MIG, SMAW, laser, resistance.  
- No pre-heat below 5 mm; over 5 mm pre-heat 75 °C to avoid centre-line cracking.  
- Use 308L or 309L filler to regain corrosion resistance in weld zone; 201-grade consumables also available.  
- Heat-input &lt;1.0 kJ/mm; inter-pass ≤150 °C to prevent sigma phase.  
- Post-weld cleaning: pickle paste 20 min, rinse + passivate; mechanical brushing with stainless steel brush acceptable for non-critical joints.

## 11. Storage & Maintenance Guidelines
- Store indoors at &lt;60 % RH; if outdoor unavoidable, elevate 100 mm, tarp with ventilated PE cloth.  
- Avoid chloride-containing detergents; use 1 % citric acid for heat-tint removal.  
- Annual pressure-wash (fresh water) coastal installations; railway stations every 6 months.  
- Scratches: use non-metallic pad + mild abrasive, wipe along grain; deeper gouges TIG-fill with 308L, re-pickle.

## 12. Frequently Asked Questions
**Q1: Will 201 rust in my kitchen?**  
A: No, provided you rinse chloride-rich sauces and avoid prolonged salt-water immersion. Normal cooking vapours are harmless.

**Q2: Can I use 201 instead of 304 for outdoor signs?**  
A: Yes in urban areas; within 2 km of shore upgrade to 316 or wash monthly.

**Q3: Is 201 food-safe?**  
A: Absolutely—complies with GB 4806.9-2023, FDA 21 CFR 175.300, EU 1935/2004. Cr & Ni migration well below limits.

**Q4: Does 201 require special welding rods?**  
A: Standard 308L works; for colour-match use 201-type consumables. Maintain low heat-input and clean afterwards.

**Q5: How does the deep-draw performance compare with 304?**  
A: Limiting drawing ratio 2.15 vs 2.20 for 304—difference negligible for most cookware. Use BA finish with light forming lube.
