
# 304 Stainless Steel – The World's Most Versatile Austenitic Workhorse for Hygiene, Strength & Value

304 Stainless Steel is the benchmark austenitic alloy that food, chemical, architectural and engineering professionals specify when they need proven corrosion resistance, formability and value in one material. Containing 18–20 % chromium and 8–10.5 % nickel with carbon held below 0.08 %, UNS S30400 delivers a stable, non-magnetic lattice that withstands oxidizing acids, urban atmospheres and repeated sterilization cycles. Compared with 201/202 grades, 304 offers twice the chloride resistance; against 316 it provides comparable performance in mild environments at 6–8 % lower cost. From brewery tanks to skyscraper façades, 304 Stainless Steel remains the first-choice "workhorse" for hygienic, durable and cost-effective fabrication.

### Easy Welding & Fabrication
- No pre-heat required; compatible with 308L or 308LSi filler—multi-pass TIG seams retain 90 % parent-metal strength.
- Post-weld cleaning with citric acid restores passive film in 20 min, meeting FDA hygiene rules.

### High & Low Temperature Stability
- Oxidation resistance to 870 °C—perfect for oven linings and exhaust manifolds.
- Charpy V-notch > 150 J at –196 °C—preferred for LNG pipe supports and cryogenic vessels.

### Hygiene & Food-Grade Safety
- Chromium oxide surface meets EU 1935/2004 and FDA 21 CFR for direct food contact; easy-to-clean finish reduces bacterial counts by 75 % vs painted carbon steel.

## Manufacturing Process & Quality Assurance

We melt 304 Stainless Steel in an electric-arc furnace, refine with AOD to reduce carbon below 0.03 % for 304L, then continuous-cast into slabs or billets. Hot rolling to strip or plate is followed by annealing at 1 010 °C and pickling in HNO₃/HF to remove oxide. ## Maintenance & Care Tips

Routine cleaning with warm water and a neutral detergent keeps 304 Stainless Steel bright; avoid steel wool or chloride bleach. For stubborn stains, use 1 % citric acid or a dedicated stainless polish, then rinse and wipe dry. Periodic passivation (2 % nitric or citric) restores the passive film in aggressive environments. Store sheets vertically on plastic-separated racks to prevent carbon-steel contamination.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q:** Is 304 Stainless Steel food-safe?  
**A:** Yes—its 18 % Cr meets FDA 21 CFR and EU 1935/2004 for neutral-pH food contact; for high-chloride sauces specify 316L.

**Q:** Difference from 316 in corrosion resistance?  
**A:** 316 adds 2 % Mo, doubling chloride pitting resistance; in urban or indoor service, 304 performs identically at lower cost.

**Q:** Can I use 304 outdoors near the ocean?  
**A:** Up to 500 m from shore is acceptable if regularly washed; closer proximity or splash zones require 316L or duplex.
